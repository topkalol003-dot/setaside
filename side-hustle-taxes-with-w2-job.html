Side hustle taxes when you also have a W-2 job

Your employer withholds tax from your paycheck, but nobody withholds anything from your side income. Worse, that side income is taxed at your highest rate, because your salary already used up the lower brackets.

How much to set aside: 2027 examples

A single filer with a $60,000 salary whose employer withholds the right amount for that salary, plus side-hustle profit:

Side profitExtra federal taxSet asidePer quarter
$5,000$1,26425.3%$316
$10,000$2,59526.0%$649
$20,000$6,05330.3%$1,513
$30,000$9,51031.7%$2,378

Federal only (self-employment tax plus income tax), 2027 IRS brackets and standard deduction, no other income or credits. State tax is extra.

Compare: someone with only $30,000 of freelance profit and no job needs to set aside about 17.9%. The same $30,000 on top of a salary needs about 31.7%.

Why it's higher than you expect

  • Self-employment tax still applies to side profit: about 14.1% of every dollar (15.3% on 92.35% of profit), on top of the Social Security and Medicare already taken from your paycheck. The Social Security part stops only once salary plus side income passes the 2027 wage base of $192,300.
  • No standard deduction left. Your salary already used it, so every side dollar is taxable.
  • Higher bracket. With a $60,000 salary, side income lands in the 12% and then 22% bracket in 2027.

Two ways to pay it

Option 1: Quarterly estimated payments

Pay the IRS four times a year through IRS Direct Pay (choose "Estimated tax" and Form 1040-ES). You generally need to do this if you'll owe $1,000 or more when you file. See the 2027 due dates.

Option 2: Extra withholding at your day job

On a new Form W-4, put an extra amount per paycheck on line 4(c). For the $30,000 side-profit example with 26 paychecks a year, that's about $366 extra per paycheck. Two advantages:

  • It's automatic. No dates to remember.
  • Withholding counts as paid evenly through the year, even if you increase it in the fall. That can fix an earlier shortfall that late estimated payments can't (see the safe harbor rule).

The downside: if your side income drops, you've over-withheld until you change your W-4 again. Many people combine both: some extra withholding, plus an estimated payment after a big month.

Run your own numbers: open the free calculator, enter your side profit, then under "More options" add your W-2 wages and the federal tax withheld (from your pay stub's year-to-date figure, projected to year-end).

Don't forget

  • Deduct side-hustle expenses: software, equipment, a share of your phone, mileage. Tax is on profit, not revenue.
  • State tax usually works the same way: no withholding on side income, so set aside your state's rate too.
  • Keep the money separate. A savings account just for taxes, topped up each time a client pays, makes April uneventful.
