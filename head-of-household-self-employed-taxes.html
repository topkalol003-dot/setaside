Head of Household and self-employed: how much to set aside

If you're unmarried, pay most of the costs of your home, and have a qualifying person living with you (often a child), you may file as Head of Household. For a freelancer or 1099 worker, that status lowers both the income tax and the amount you need to put aside from every payment.

Why Head of Household costs less

  • Bigger standard deduction: $24,925 for 2027, versus $16,600 for Single.
  • Wider low brackets: the 10% bracket goes up to $18,250 of taxable income (Single: $12,800), and the 12% bracket up to $69,650 (Single: $52,025).
  • Self-employment tax doesn't change. It's about 14.1% of profit for everyone, which is why the savings are in income tax only.

Do you qualify? The basic test

Generally, all of these must be true:

  1. You're unmarried (or "considered unmarried") on the last day of the year.
  2. You paid more than half the cost of keeping up your home for the year.
  3. A qualifying person lived with you for more than half the year. A dependent parent is an exception and doesn't have to live with you.

The rules have details (divorced and separated parents, who counts as a qualifying person). Check IRS Publication 501 or the IRS Interactive Tax Assistant if you're unsure.

Quarterly payments work the same way

Head of Household filers follow the same estimated-tax rules as everyone else: pay if you'll owe $1,000 or more, on the four 2027 due dates, and use the safe harbor to avoid the underpayment penalty. With a $50,000 profit and no credits, that's about $2,321 per quarter.
