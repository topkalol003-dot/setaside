The estimated tax safe harbor rule, explained

You can't know your exact tax until the year is over. The safe harbor rule gives you a target you can know in advance. Hit it on time and you generally won't owe an underpayment penalty, even if you still owe tax in April.

The rule in one sentence

You generally avoid the underpayment penalty if your withholding plus on-time estimated payments add up to at least the smaller of:

  • 90% of the tax you will owe for this year, or
  • 100% of the total tax on last year's return. This becomes 110% if last year's adjusted gross income (AGI) was over $150,000 ($75,000 if married filing separately).

Last year's return has to cover a full 12 months. You also don't owe a penalty if this year's tax after withholding is under $1,000.

Why last year's number is so useful

This year's tax is a guess until December. Last year's tax is a fixed number printed on your return (the "total tax" line of Form 1040). Paying a quarter of it on each due date is a target you can plan around from January, even if your income jumps.

Example 1: income is growing

Maya is single. Her 2026 return shows total tax of $8,000 and AGI of $90,000. In 2027 her freelance business takes off and she expects about $14,000 of tax.

  • 90% of this year: $12,600
  • 100% of last year: $8,000 (AGI was under $150,000)

Her safe harbor is the smaller amount: $8,000, or $2,000 per quarter. If she pays that on time, she generally owes no penalty and settles the remaining ~$6,000 when she files in April 2028. She should still keep that $6,000 aside in savings.

Example 2: higher earner

Jordan's 2026 AGI was $170,000 and total tax was $30,000. Because AGI was over $150,000, the prior-year target is 110%: $33,000, or $8,250 a quarter, unless 90% of this year's tax turns out lower.

Example 3: income is falling

If you expect a much smaller year, 90% of this year's tax may be the lower number, and you can pay less. The risk: if you underestimate, you can fall short of both targets. Re-check your estimate before each due date.

Good to know

  • Timing matters. Safe harbor works per installment: each payment should be made by its due date. Paying everything in January doesn't fix a missed April.
  • W-2 withholding is treated as paid evenly through the year, even if it happened in December. Raising withholding at a day job late in the year can fill a gap.
  • Uneven income? The annualized income installment method (Form 2210, Schedule AI) can lower the required early payments if you earned most of your money late in the year.
  • Safe harbor is not the same as "nothing more to pay". It protects you from the penalty, not from the tax itself.
