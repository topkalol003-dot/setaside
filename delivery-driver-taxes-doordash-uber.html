Delivery driver taxes: how much to set aside in 2027

If you deliver for DoorDash, Uber Eats, Instacart, Grubhub or similar apps, you're self-employed: nothing is withheld, and you owe self-employment tax on top of income tax. The good news is that your miles are probably your biggest deduction, and they change the answer a lot.

The mileage deduction changes everything

Tax is on profit, not on what the app pays you. With the standard mileage method you deduct a fixed amount per business mile instead of gas, repairs and depreciation. The IRS rate for the second half of 2026 is 76 cents per mile (72.5 cents for January–June 2026). The 2027 rate is usually announced in December; the examples below use 76 cents as an estimate.

Without tracked miles, the same $30,000 driver would owe about 2.5 times more. That's why a mileage log is worth more than almost anything else you can do.

Track miles the right way

  • Log every business mile: from when you head out to accept orders, between pickups and drop-offs, and back. Commuting from home to a regular workplace doesn't count, but most delivery driving does. The apps' own mileage summaries often only count miles with an order, which can undercount.
  • Keep a contemporaneous log: date, miles, purpose. A mileage app or a simple spreadsheet both work.
  • Pick a method in your first year: to use the standard mileage rate for a car, you generally need to choose it in the first year you use the car for business.
  • Other deductions: the business share of your phone and plan, insulated bags, parking and tolls while working.

Do you have to pay quarterly?

If you expect to owe $1,000 or more for the year after withholding, the IRS expects estimated payments. As the table shows, even part-time drivers often cross that line. Pay through IRS Direct Pay ("Estimated tax", Form 1040-ES). The 2027 due dates are April 15, June 15 and September 15, 2027, and January 18, 2028.

Driving on top of a day job? Your delivery profit is taxed at your higher bracket. See side hustle taxes with a W-2 job.

What the 1099 shows (and doesn't)

Apps send a 1099-NEC or 1099-K for your earnings. It shows money in, not your miles or costs. You report income and deductions yourself on Schedule C, and self-employment tax on Schedule SE. Even if you don't receive a form, the income is still taxable.
