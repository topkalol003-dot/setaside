Quarterly estimated taxes 2027: due dates, amounts and how to pay

When you work for yourself, nobody takes tax out of your pay. The IRS still wants it during the year, in four installments. Here is how that works for the 2027 tax year.

Who has to pay estimated taxes?

Generally, you need to make estimated payments if you expect to owe $1,000 or more in federal tax for the year after subtracting withholding and refundable credits. That covers most freelancers, 1099 contractors, gig workers and side hustlers with real profit, because no employer is withholding tax for them.

If you also have a W-2 job, the tax withheld from your paycheck counts toward what you owe. Sometimes raising your W-4 withholding at that job is enough, and you can skip separate estimated payments.

The four 2027 due dates

Q4Sep 1 – Dec 31, 2027January 18, 2028*

*January 15, 2028 is a Saturday and January 17 is Martin Luther King Jr. Day, so the payment moves to the next business day. Confirm on IRS.gov closer to the date. The "quarters" are not equal: Q2 is two months and Q4 is four.

Still settling 2026? The last 2026 payment is due January 15, 2027.

How much should each payment be?

Your yearly tax as a self-employed person has two main parts:

  1. Self-employment tax: 15.3% on 92.35% of your net profit. That is Social Security (12.4%, up to the 2027 wage base of $192,300) plus Medicare (2.9%, no cap).
  2. Federal income tax on your taxable income, after the standard deduction ($16,600 single or $33,200 married filing jointly for 2027) and a deduction for half of your self-employment tax.

Add them up, subtract any W-2 withholding, and divide by four. For example, a single freelancer with $60,000 of net profit in 2027 and no other income owes about $8,478 in self-employment tax and $4,443 in federal income tax: $12,921 for the year, or about $3,230 per quarter. That is roughly 21.5% of profit.

Your numbers will differ. Run them in the free calculator. It takes under a minute and nothing you type leaves your browser.

You do not have to pay exactly 25% of the year's tax each time if your income is uneven. Paying at least the safe harbor amount on time generally protects you from the underpayment penalty, even if you owe more in April.

How to pay the IRS

  • IRS Direct Pay: free bank transfer from irs.gov/payments. Choose "Estimated Tax" and Form 1040-ES for the right year.
  • Your IRS Online Account: also free, and it shows the payments you have already made.
  • EFTPS: free, needs enrollment in advance.
  • Debit or credit card: through IRS-approved processors, which charge a fee.
  • Check by mail with a Form 1040-ES voucher.

Keep the confirmation number. You will need the total of your estimated payments when you file your return.

What if you miss a payment or pay too little?

The IRS charges an underpayment penalty, which works like interest on the amount that was late or short, for the time it was late. It is usually smaller than people fear, but it adds up. If you missed a quarter, pay as soon as you can to stop it growing. Form 2210 is where the penalty is calculated, and its annualized income method can help if most of your income came late in the year.
